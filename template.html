Classical & Traditional 01
Biennial  ·  Exhibition Preview
75 Museum Shows to See in Spring 2026 — Venice Leads

ArtNews dropped its essential spring preview this morning, headlined by the 61st Venice Biennale’s main exhibition In Minor Keys — 111 artists centered on diaspora, memory, and procession. The Marcel Duchamp retrospective at MoMA is the other can’t-miss. The list is worth reading slowly.

artnews.com  ↑

Venice every two years sets the tone for what institutions will be programming and what critics will be thinking about for the next 18 months. This year’s theme pulls toward the intimate and the diasporic — a counterpoint to the spectacle years. In Minor Keys is a title that rewards attention.
Painting  ·  Sculpture David Cohen: Têtes et Mots — Paris Opens Tomorrow

Opening Tuesday at Galerie Rachel Hardouin, this show runs through April 11 and puts paintings, sculptures, and ceramics in direct conversation around the human figure and the written word. Cohen’s work has an old-world density that feels out of step with everything — in the best way.

Digital & Generative 02
AI Editorial  ·  Fashion
INSIGNIFICANT — Midjourney Fashion Inside a Misty Forest

A collaboration between AI Design Awards and Topaz Labs, INSIGNIFICANT is a fashion editorial made entirely with Midjourney and Topaz, shot in a misty forest that doesn’t exist. It’s a reminder that the uncanny is still the sharpest tool generative artists have.

threads.com  ↑

The images land somewhere between haute couture editorial and Caspar David Friedrich — figures dwarfed by atmospheric wilderness that has no address. A two-tool pipeline with a genuine point of view. The collaboration is a proof-of-concept for what AI creative work looks like when craft is the intention, not the accident.
AI Animation  ·  Documentary BBC Witness History Goes AI-Animated

The BBC World Service announced five AI-animated adaptations of Witness History episodes — archival radio turned into moving image via generative tools. It raises thorny questions about animation, authorship, and what historical footage “is,” but visually the results are arresting.

Street & Urban 03
Mural  ·  Community Art
Alysa Liu Gets Her Mural — Oakland Comes Through

Illuminaries just unveiled a massive mural of two-time Olympic gold medalist Alysa Liu on the corner of Telegraph Ave and 43rd St in Oakland. The focal image is Liu mid-thizz face — that Bay Area expression of pride — which makes the whole thing feel like the neighborhood claiming her, not the other way around.

nbcbayarea.com  ↑

Public murals at their best don’t just decorate — they locate. This one puts Alysa Liu squarely in a specific geography, a specific cultural expression, a specific community that watched her win. The thizz face is not incidental. It’s the whole argument about what community art actually does when it’s done right.
Chicano Art  ·  Preservation Barrio Logan Sounds the Alarm on Its Murals

San Diego’s Chicano Park — a National Historic Landmark — is facing a familiar threat: development pressure erasing the murals that made it sacred in the first place. The community response is fierce and worth watching. A story about what public art actually costs to keep.

USA Today readers voted Philly the top city for street art for the second year running. The Mural Arts Program has made the city a genuine open-air gallery — over 4,000 murals at last count. Worth revisiting if you haven’t been paying attention.

Photography 04
Street Photography  ·  Awards
30 Winners from the 2026 Leica Street Photo Awards

121Clicks put together a full gallery of all 30 winning images from this year’s Leica Street Photo Awards — entries from India, Poland, the UK, China, and more. No single aesthetic dominates, which is exactly the point. Bookmark this one for a slow scroll.

121clicks.com  ↑

The diversity of approach — from classically composed to formally radical — tells you something about the health of street photography as a practice. It’s not homogenizing. It’s expanding. The Leica imprimatur still carries weight, and this year’s winners show why: the bar is genuine, not institutional.
Nature Photography  ·  Gold Award “Water Ballet” — A Giraffe, a Waterhole, Perfect Light

Singapore permanent resident Vaidehi Chandrasekar took gold at the 2026 World Nature Photography Awards with a shot of a giraffe near a watering hole titled Water Ballet. Shot at 1/1000 sec, it captures a rare behavioral moment wrapped in golden-hour backlight. One of those images that earns its prize.

Design & Typography 05
Typography  ·  Trends Report
Top 10 Typography Trends Dominating 2026

Published this morning, Authentype’s trend report flags the big shift: type families are increasingly being designed with Latin, Arabic, Cyrillic, and CJK scripts simultaneously — equal weight, no afterthought localization. It’s a genuine structural change in how type is made.

authentype.com  ↑

The historical model — design for Latin, adapt for others — is collapsing under market pressure and design conscience simultaneously. The next decade of type design will look structurally different from the last. If you work with type in any capacity, this report is required reading.
Design Research  ·  AI Figma’s State of Design 2026 — Craft Is Still Everything

Figma’s annual report lands with a headline that will annoy AI boosters: 57% of designers still define craft as visual polish and attention to detail, not prompting speed. The AI conversation in design is real, but it’s running into the same wall — taste is not automatable. Yet.
