Mark Rothko’s Artistic Evolution Traced in Florence Exhibition

An exhibition in Florence, curated by his son, traces Mark Rothko’s complete artistic journey from early figurative pieces to his iconic color field paintings. Featuring over 70 works, the show draws a full arc of a career that changed how we think about color as emotion.

Numéro Netherlands

Rothko’s 1950 visit to Florence was pivotal — the frescoes in the Brancacci Chapel reportedly hit him like a physical force. Seeing early work alongside the late color fields, with that context, reframes what he was actually doing with all that silence.
Fine Art Beverly Buchanan’s Architecture of Care

Beverly Buchanan’s work is having a deserved moment — this piece centers her practice of building small shack sculptures as a form of witness and care for the rural Black South she grew up in. Her architecture isn’t about form; it’s about who gets remembered.

Hyperallergic Sculpture In ‘Life Forms,’ Janny Baek Imagines a Speculative Landscape

Baek’s ceramic sculptures reach somewhere between organism and architecture — biomorphic forms that feel like they belong to a future ecology we haven’t arrived at yet. The show is called Life Forms for a reason: these pieces pulse.

Colossal Studio Visit A Visit to Tomás Saraceno’s Berlin Studio Delves into a Deeply Empathetic Practice

Art21 goes inside Saraceno’s Berlin studio, where spider webs, aerosolar balloons, and questions about cohabitation with non-human life are all equally serious materials. His practice is one of the most genuinely unusual in contemporary art.

Colossal
Visual History A Visual Journey Through 150 Years of the Legal Aid Society

Documentary photographs and archival images trace the Legal Aid Society’s 150-year history — a visual record of justice work that rarely gets treated as art history. The images are stark and honest in ways institutional photography usually isn’t.

Hyperallergic Curatorial On Curating Carnage

A sharp essay on what it means to exhibit images of violence and death — who gets to decide what “worthy” suffering looks like in a gallery context. It’s the kind of piece that makes you reconsider every war photography show you’ve ever walked through.

Hyperallergic Craft & Object You’ll Need a Magnifying Glass to Read Some of the World’s Smallest Books at the V&A

The V&A is showing miniature books from the National Art Library — some smaller than a thumbnail, yet containing complete texts, illustrations, and binding work executed at a scale that requires magnification to appreciate. Object-making at its most obsessive and quietly radical.

The Hidden Cost of AI Design Tools — What We’re Outsourcing Without Noticing

The real cost of AI design tools isn’t the subscription — it’s the slow erosion of judgment. This essay argues that designers are outsourcing decisions they don’t realize they’re outsourcing: aesthetic intuition, constraint navigation, the hard-won ability to know when something is wrong.

UX Collective

The piece doesn’t argue against AI tools — it argues for awareness of what atrophies when you let them do too much. The designers who’ll win are the ones who keep their judgment sharp while using the speed. That tension is real and worth sitting with.
Brand Identity Glee Brand Identity by Ahmed Yossry

Ahmed Yossry’s Glee identity is the kind of brand work that looks deceptively simple until you notice how precisely every decision is made — color, type, motion all locked to a single emotional register. Clean, confident, and genuinely joyful without being saccharine.

Abduzeedo Design History What Happened to the Car Designed for Women, by Women?

In the 1990s, Volvo assembled an all-female design team to build a car that addressed every friction women reported in automotive design. The result was visionary — and shelved. This piece recovers what happened and why it matters that the industry forgot it.

UX Collective Product & UX The First Fruit of the Google–Apple AI Pact

The Google-Apple AI deal is producing its first visible interface decisions — and they reveal how both companies are carving up the assistant layer of mobile. The UX choices embedded in these first outputs say more about the partnership’s real terms than any press release.

UX Collective
Design Process ChatGPT Design Brief: 20 Prompts That Actually Help

A practical collection of prompts built around real design workflow stages — brief writing, concept exploration, copy direction, critique framing. Tested against actual projects, not synthetic demos. The kind of resource that acknowledges AI is a tool, not a replacement for knowing what you want.

Abduzeedo UX Strategy Why B2B UX Features Fail

B2B products keep shipping features users don’t adopt — and this piece traces why: the wrong people define success, enterprise buyers and actual users want different things, and “feature” and “solution” get confused constantly. Useful diagnosis for anyone building tools for businesses.
