Paint & Canvas — Lead
Lucian Freud Mastered the Art of Lostness

A new essay on Lucian Freud argues that his real achievement wasn’t hyperrealism — it was mastering the feeling of being existentially adrift and putting it directly onto flesh. His figures don’t just occupy space; they seem uncertain they have the right to.

Hyperallergic

Most painters trying to capture psychological states lean on expression. Freud did the opposite — he found it in stillness, weight, in skin that looks like it’s carrying too much. That instinct is what this piece unpacks. Useful for anyone working figuratively.
Photography Dani Guindo’s Aerial Photos Reveal the Ghostly Outline of an Icelandic Glacier

Shot from the air, Guindo’s images of retreating Icelandic glaciers read less like documentation and more like monochromatic abstract paintings — pale forms dissolving into dark terrain. Climate grief rendered as pure composition.

Colossal Exhibition March Avery, ‘Form into Color’ at Esther Schipper

March Avery’s show presents a lifetime of turning figures and landscapes into saturated blocks of pure color. Less concerned with representation than with the pleasure of paint itself — a painter who treats the canvas as something to inhabit, daily.

Ocula Portrait / Natural History Spectral Birds of New Zealand Find New Life in Fiona Pardington’s Portraits

Pardington photographs extinct and endangered New Zealand birds using historical museum specimens, creating large-format portraits that sit somewhere between natural history and mourning. The birds feel present and gone at the same time.

Colossal
Exhibition Gavin Turk: The Escapologist Beyond The Threshold

Turk’s oil paintings in this show invite viewers to question the stability of images themselves — what we see, what we believe, and what happens when belief wavers. Reckoning with appearance as a subject, not a technique.

Artlyst Campaign / Visual Design Mischief’s Genius Ads for NPR Provoke Urgent Questions About the Right to Information

Ad agency Mischief turns NPR’s logo into a series of pointed questions about what curiosity means when access to information is threatened. Conceptually tight, visually minimal, and genuinely uncomfortable — advertising that actually earns the word “campaign.”

Colossal Criticism Awards Season and the Management of Cultural Power

A sharp piece on how awards cycles function not as meritocracies but as mechanisms for managing who gets to matter in the cultural conversation. Worth reading if you’ve ever wondered why the same names keep circulating.

The Creative Mind — Lead
Hugo Rocci Paints the Tools We Forget to See

Hugo Rocci’s paintings take ordinary tools — the kind that accumulate in studios and garages without ever being looked at — and subjects them to the same intensity a portraitist gives a face. The result is a strange tenderness for functional objects.

Creative Boom

There’s a whole tradition of still life as a way to slow down and pay attention, and Rocci is clearly working in it — but the choice of tools, rather than fruit or flowers, gives the work a specific register. Worth looking at closely if you paint objects.
Illustration / Collaboration Linda Merad Dives into an Upside-Down Ocean for Hermès

Illustrator Linda Merad inverts the ocean for a Hermès commission, creating a world where marine life floats upward into sky. It’s the kind of brief that could go mannered fast — she keeps it genuinely strange.

Creative Boom Character Design Meet Baumann: Craig Boylan’s Plastic Man Who Can Do Just About Anything

Craig Boylan has spent years developing Baumann, a single character built from the logic of plastic — malleable, absurd, endlessly adaptable. A case study in how a constrained character concept can become a full creative system.

Creative Boom Essay When You Don’t Meet Your Own Artistic Goals

An honest reflection on the gap between what we intend to make and what life actually allows. The author offers practical reframes — not toxic positivity, but real strategies for artists navigating creative stalls.

Medium
Branding Barkhouse Hotel: How Do You Brand a Service for a Client Who Can’t Read?

Crown Creative’s identity for a luxury dog hotel rests entirely on visual and tactile cues — because the real client, the dog, can’t engage with language. A smart brief answered with equal smartness.

Creative Boom Interview Returning McKenzie Ellis (Mothica) on the Importance of Enjoying the Process

Mothica discusses her multidisciplinary practice across music, visuals, and design — and why staying present in the work matters more than optimizing for the outcome. Honest about the cost of not doing that.

Interface & UX — Lead
Web Shaders: The Next Big Trend in UI Design

Abduzeedo makes the case that GPU-driven GLSL shaders are moving from creative coding novelty into mainstream UI — fluid, light-reactive surfaces that no CSS gradient can replicate. The argument is backed with actual examples rather than vibes.

Abduzeedo

If you’ve been watching high-end agency sites over the last year, you’ve seen this shift already. Shaders give designers a texture and motion vocabulary that nothing else does. The question is whether tooling will democratize it fast enough to matter.
Branding Splinker ID: Sports Branding for the Athlete Era

A branding case study that steps away from team-centric sports identity toward something built around individual athletes — personal mark-making for an era when the athlete is the media property. Clean execution, clear rationale.

Abduzeedo Trends Experience Design Trends 2026: Designing for Intent

The shift from fixed interfaces to intent-aware layouts — where the page structure actually changes based on inferred user goals — is the most consequential pattern in this year’s UX conversation. More than personalization; it’s architectural.

UX Collective Design Systems The Future of Design Systems: Adaptive Interfaces and AI Integration

Design systems are becoming less about static component libraries and more about adaptive orchestration — systems that evolve with product context and get AI assistance for consistency audits and accessibility improvements.

WeAreBrain
Tools Adobe Photoshop AI Assistant Brings Conversational Image Editing

Adobe’s new AI assistant integrates conversational commands directly into Photoshop’s editing flow, letting users describe adjustments in plain language instead of hunting through panels. Early testing suggests it actually works for routine tasks — which is more than most AI tool integrations can claim.
