Longevity and Obsoletion Impress Upon Alexander Endrullat’s Intaglio Prints

Endrullat’s prints sit at the intersection of things that endure and things that become obsolete — the grid-like precision of intaglio suits a practice obsessed with systems, time, and what survives when everything else fades. These aren’t decorative objects; they feel like records.

Colossal

Intaglio has been around for centuries, and Endrullat uses that weight deliberately — a medium associated with durability making work about the things that don’t last. That tension is where all the energy is.
Photography ‘A Language We Share’ Traces a Photographic Lineage Between Gordon Parks and Beverly Price

This exhibition draws a direct line between Gordon Parks and Beverly Price — two photographers across different eras who both used the camera as a tool of advocacy. Seeing their work together makes clear how much one set the grammar the other inherited.

Colossal Sculpture Historic Architecture Emerges from Stone in Matthew Simmonds’ Ethereal Sculptures

Simmonds carves interior architectural spaces — vaulted corridors, cathedral apses — directly into blocks of marble and stone, leaving you genuinely uncertain whether you’re looking at a building or a miniature world. The technical control is extraordinary; the feeling it produces is something else entirely.

Colossal Arts & Culture Brian Eno and 200+ Artists Urge British Museum to “Stop Erasing Palestine”

Over 200 artists and cultural figures have signed an open letter pressing the British Museum to stop what they describe as the erasure of Palestinian cultural identity from its exhibitions and collection framing. The letter sharpens a long-running debate about how major institutions navigate political pressure.

Boom Brief #7: How You Branded Petal & Stem, a Florist Built on Friendship

Creative Boom opens up a real branding brief — a florist born from friendship, built on texture and warmth rather than market positioning — and shows how designers responded. The answers reveal what good identity work actually looks like when the brief has real emotional stakes.

Creative Boom

Community briefs like this are rare. Instead of aspirational case studies, you get actual process from working designers who had to make something emotionally true, not just visually competitive. Worth studying if you do brand work.
Process & Craft The Creative Process: A Journey of Exploration and Intuition

This piece argues that the journey through a blank canvas — including the moments of struggle and genuine discovery — holds more value than the finished outcome. Exploration and intuition, not optimization, are what the creative process is actually for.

GSAP Web Animations and the Personal Site Revival

GSAP is back at the center of the personal site renaissance — designers who got bored with static pages and template monotony are returning to hand-crafted animation as the primary mode of expression. This piece tracks the revival and the tools making it happen.

Abduzeedo

There’s something specifically appealing about GSAP right now: it sits in the sweet spot between what CSS can do and what a full WebGL setup requires. Personal sites built with it have a distinctly authored feel that frameworks rarely produce.
Design & AI The Honest Designer’s Guide to AI: What It Can Do, What It Can’t, and Where You Still Win

A clear-eyed breakdown of where AI tools genuinely help designers and where they consistently fall short — written for practitioners, not hype cycles. The “where you still win” section is the most useful part: specificity, taste, and the ability to identify bad outputs remain irreplaceable.

Abduzeedo UX & Product The First Fruit of the Google–Apple AI Pact

The Google-Apple AI partnership is producing its first visible product changes — and this piece analyzes what those UX decisions reveal about how both companies intend to divide the AI assistant territory. The design implications are more significant than they first appear.
