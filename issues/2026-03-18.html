Paint & Canvas — Lead
Myth, Masks, and LEGO: Ekow Nimako’s Elaborate Afrofuturistic Sculptures

Ghanaian-Canadian sculptor Ekow Nimako builds large-scale mythologies entirely from black LEGO bricks, constructing works that reference West African cosmology and contemporary Black speculative fiction simultaneously. The sculptures operate at a scale where you forget the material entirely — until you get close and the individual bricks reassert themselves, and the labor becomes inseparable from the meaning.

Colossal

There is something specific here for anyone thinking about materials and constraint: Nimako has found a vocabulary inside one of the most restrictive formal systems imaginable and is using it to say things that have nothing to do with the system. That gap between material and meaning is worth sitting with.
Digital & Animation Artists Set Islamic Futurism Into Motion

Contemporary artists using animation, digital imagery, and installation to build Islamic futurist visual languages that exist outside both Western science fiction and nostalgic traditionalism. The work is world-building more than commentary — proposing aesthetic futures, not just critiquing present ones.

Hyperallergic Art History & Culture The Canonization of Frida Kahlo

An examination of how Frida Kahlo’s image has been absorbed into mainstream culture — tea towels, tote bags, corporate campaigns — and what that canonization erases. The specific, difficult, political content of her work is exactly what disappears when it becomes decoration, and this piece names that precisely.

Hyperallergic Photography Photographer Dr. Elliot McGucken Seizes a Rare Superbloom in Death Valley

Dr. Elliot McGucken documented a rare desert superbloom in Death Valley, capturing hillsides blanketed in wildflowers that appear only when winter rains align just right. The photographs are luminous and slightly surreal — landscapes that make you wonder if the camera is exaggerating or actually underselling what was there.

Colossal
Exhibition & Events Your Go-To Guide to NYC’s Spring Art Fairs

A comprehensive guide to the wave of art fairs hitting New York this spring, from the institutional anchors to the scrappier satellite shows that often surface the more interesting work. Useful navigation for anyone trying to make intentional choices in a week that can otherwise turn into expensive, exhausting noise.

Hyperallergic Museums & Institutions The New New Museum

A critical look at the recently renovated New Museum building and what its reopening reveals about how contemporary art institutions are positioning themselves in a city where real estate and cultural prestige are increasingly inseparable. The piece has opinions, and earns them.

Hyperallergic Artists to Watch 5 Artists on Our Radar in March 2026

Artsy’s monthly roundup includes a review of “House Made of Dawn: Art by Native Americans, 1880–Now,” an exhibition reframing American art history by centering Indigenous artists as key contributors to modern and contemporary practice. Strong picks across media and geography, with a consistent editorial commitment to artists outside the usual institutional pipeline.

The Creative Mind — Lead
Filmmaker Arielle Knight on Making Work That Cuts Through the Noise

Arielle Knight talks about documentary and essayistic filmmaking that connects with audiences not through spectacle but through precise attention to what most filmmakers edit out. She is specific about process: the role of play, the importance of not knowing what you’re making before you start, and how she finds the signal in a lot of noise.

The Creative Independent

The interviews in this series tend to be most useful when the subject can describe the exact mechanism of their creative process rather than its results — what they actually do, not how it reads in retrospect. Knight sounds like she can make that distinction, which makes this worth the read.
Illustration & Craft Mapping the Star Wars Galaxy with Illustrator Tim McDonagh

Tim McDonagh created a massive, detailed cartographic illustration of the Star Wars galaxy, applying the visual language and precision of historical cartography to fictional geography. The project is a good look at what happens when a serious commercial illustrator brings the full weight of their technical craft to franchise material — the result is more rigorous, and more interesting, than it has any reason to be.

Interface & UX — Lead
Forget Your “Lovable” Products; the Real Leverage Point Was Always Learning

The piece argues that “lovable” has become a design industry mantra that substitutes for harder thinking about what products are actually for — and that the real leverage point has always been creating systems that help users learn and grow. A skeptical, rigorous read for anyone who has sat through a “delight the user” brief without being able to articulate why it felt insufficient.

UX Collective

The best design criticism tends to name something practitioners already feel but haven’t had language for. If “lovable” has been bothering you as a design goal, this likely gives you the frame for why — and what the more honest alternative looks like.
UX Patterns No Further Questions… Please.

A direct critique of onboarding flows and interview-style product experiences that ask too many questions, examining how excessive data collection friction drives users away before they reach the value. The proposed fixes are concrete, the diagnosis sharper than most pieces in this genre.

UX Collective Algorithms & Feed Design How Werewolves Killed My Pinterest

A case study in how recommendation algorithms optimized for engagement gradually destroy the utility that made a platform worth using in the first place. The werewolf example is specific and documented: a single interest colonizing a feed once the algorithm locks onto a signal and can’t let go.

UX Collective Career & Practice How Accepting “Just Build This Thing” Can Hurt Your Design Career

An argument for why accepting implementation-only briefs — without discovery, without strategy, without pushback — is what keeps designers at the execution tier rather than advancing to roles with more consequence. Practical and clear-eyed about the tradeoff designers make when they optimize for getting along.

UX Collective
Studio & Practice MONOLOG Creative Studio: Huy Nguyen, AI and Webflow

Huy Nguyen runs MONOLOG as a solo creative studio, using AI tools and Webflow to ship high-quality branded work at a pace that was previously impossible without a team. A case study in what a skilled individual practitioner can now actually deliver, and what that means for how studios are structured and priced.

Abduzeedo Branding & Identity BBH Rebrand: Typeface and Zag Identity Design

The BBH rebrand is built around a new custom typeface and an updated identity system rooted in the agency’s famous “Zag” positioning — move against the crowd, find the opposite. A rare rebrand that actually commits to its own concept rather than retreating to safe refinement.

Abduzeedo AI & Identity Design ValkaAI Brand Identity: Studio L–AB Puts AI Faces First

Studio L-AB’s identity for ValkaAI places human faces at the center of an AI product’s visual system — a deliberate pushback against the abstracted gradient aesthetics that have come to define the space. The choice reads as a bet on warmth and specificity over technological ambiguity, and it lands.
