Stephen Morrison’s Trompe-L’œil ‘Dog World’ Paintings Are Fetching

Stephen Morrison’s hyperrealist paintings trick the eye into believing dogs inhabit a world built entirely to their scale and sensibility. The trompe-l’œil technique is deployed with enough wit and precision that the work more than earns its puns.

Colossal

Trompe-l’œil painting is one of the oldest technical challenges in the medium, and Morrison uses it not for spectacle but for humor and warmth. Worth studying for the craft alone before you even get to the dogs.
Criticism White Girls and the Global South

An essay examining representation, race, and the politics of looking in contemporary art, interrogating how certain bodies become symbols in international cultural narratives. Required reading for anyone thinking about whose perspective shapes what we call fine art.

Hyperallergic AI & Authorship Is There an Ethical Path for AI Art?

Hyperallergic takes on the persistent question of whether generative AI can produce work that belongs in the conversation about art, ethics, training data, and authorship. The answer is less settled than anyone on either side would like.

Hyperallergic Exhibition The Angel of History Is Stuck in Jerusalem

An exhibition review exploring how artists grapple with history, memory, and the impossible weight of place in Jerusalem’s contemporary art scene. The work refuses easy resolution, which is exactly the point.

‘You come for the food. You return for Bonnie.’ — Kathryn Farwell on designing the relationship, not just the work

Designer Kathryn Farwell discusses her philosophy of embedding personality and long-term relationships into client work, using a restaurant branding project as the lens. The insight: the best design work is not about the artifact but the connection it creates.

Creative Boom

Most designers talk about craft. Farwell talks about loyalty. The distinction matters more than it sounds, and this interview makes the case without overselling it.

The practice of presenting speculative or unrealized concept work as finished product is under scrutiny, with designers debating where the line is between inspiration and deception. The answer has real stakes for how the profession is perceived.

Niche Design Press: A Zine for Independent Design

Niche Design Press arrives as a printed zine dedicated to independent designers working outside the mainstream, spotlighting voices and work that do not make it onto the usual aggregators. Worth supporting if you believe design criticism still matters in print.

Abduzeedo

Print design media is not dead; it just got selective. Niche Design Press is exactly what its name promises: a narrow beam aimed at the people who are actually doing interesting work away from the algorithm.
Brand Identity Unbegun Brand Identity by Bram Naus: A Logo That Never Stops Turning

Bram Naus’s identity system for Unbegun is built around motion as a core brand property, with a logo designed to be perpetually in process rather than resolved. A smart conceptual fit for a brand whose name already implies becoming.

Abduzeedo Dev Tools Motion Core: Svelte Animation Toolkit Powered by GSAP and Three.js

Motion Core packages GSAP and Three.js into a Svelte-native animation toolkit, making high-quality motion accessible to frontend developers without deep animation expertise. Worth evaluating if you are building anything interactive in Svelte.

AI & Design The Intelligence Revolution Won’t Be Televised

A long-arc take arguing that the real transformation of AI into daily life is happening slowly and invisibly, not through dramatic product launches but through accumulated automation across a thousand small decisions. The frame is useful for anyone thinking about where design work is actually going.

UX Collective AI Systems Raising the Machine

An essay on what it means to design AI systems that learn and grow, drawing parallels between parenting and the iterative process of shaping machine behavior through feedback and intention. Thought-provoking even when the metaphor strains.

As augmented reality hardware enters the consumer mainstream, UX Collective asks the question the product launches mostly ignored: are these devices designed for anyone other than able-bodied early adopters? The accessibility gap is significant and largely unaddressed.
