Get ‘Super/Natural’ Inside Judith Schaechter’s Stained Glass Sculpture

Judith Schaechter transforms stained glass from flat window panels into immersive sculptural environments you can physically enter and inhabit. The effect is a collision of medieval craft and contemporary installation that redefines what the medium can be.

Colossal

Schaechter has spent decades proving stained glass belongs in the same conversation as painting and sculpture. This installation is the fullest expression of that argument, and it makes its case through sheer sensory overload rather than polemic.

When Defeat Looked This Good: The Street Fighter Sculptures That Hit Different

A series of sculptures captures the iconic defeated poses from Street Fighter with a fidelity and material presence that elevates pixel-era nostalgia into legitimate fine art object-making. The work treats video game culture as source material worthy of the same craft attention as classical sculpture.

Creative Boom

There is a specific kind of physical comedy frozen into those defeat animations that only sculptural form can really honor. These pieces understand that the humor and the craft are not in tension with each other.
Branding How Sip Studio Built La Borosa, a Non-Alcoholic Spirit That Doesn’t Ask for Your Forgiveness

Sip Studio’s branding for La Borosa refuses the apologetic tone that plagues most non-alcoholic drinks, positioning the product as a deliberate choice rather than a compromise. The identity work is confident, adult, and refreshingly unapologetic.

Creative Boom Typography How Letters Come Alive: Atelier AAAAA’s Identity for Mixt

Atelier AAAAA builds an identity system for Mixt where the letterforms themselves become animated, responsive elements rather than static marks. The result is a brand that feels alive on screen in a way that most logotypes cannot achieve.

Figma AI Agents Now Write Directly to the Design Canvas

Figma’s AI agents can now modify design files directly on the canvas, moving beyond suggestion into active creation within the designer’s primary workspace. The shift from copilot to co-creator inside the tool itself changes the relationship between designer and software fundamentally.
