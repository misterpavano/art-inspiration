Mirrors, Iron, and Stone Conjure Ancestral Healing in Olayami Dabls’ Detroit Museum

Olayami Dabls has spent decades transforming a Detroit city block into the African Bead Museum, a sprawling outdoor installation of mirror-covered structures, painted iron, and raw stone that channels African material traditions into a living environment of cultural reclamation. The work exists outside the art world’s usual circuits, rooted entirely in community and place.

Colossal

This is the kind of artist-made space that makes institutional museums look cautious. Dabls didn’t wait for permission or a residency — he built an entire world. Mirrors as community, iron as memory, stone as ground. Worth a close look.
Photography Lisette Model’s Silenced Jazz Pictures

A look at the jazz photographs Lisette Model made in New York’s nightclubs, long suppressed and only now receiving their due. Her camera caught musicians in full abandon — images that refused the flattering formality of press photography and caught the actual heat of the music.

Hyperallergic Exhibition Giulia Andreani’s ‘Sabotage’ at Hamburger Bahnhof

Italian painter Giulia Andreani is showing ghostly figurative paintings at Hamburger Bahnhof, using archival photographs to illuminate forgotten historical narratives. The exhibition enters direct dialogue with Sigmar Polke’s past work in the same space, layering past and present with quiet authority.

ArtReview Obituary Thaddeus Mosley, Beloved Self-Taught Sculptor, Dies at 99

Pittsburgh sculptor Thaddeus Mosley, who spent 60 years carving massive abstract forms from raw wood in his basement studio, has died at 99. His work — discovered late, celebrated internationally — remains one of the most singular bodies of self-taught American sculpture.

Marie Holst Is Reweaving Tapestries for the Digital Age

Designer Marie Holst is bridging the ancient craft of tapestry with contemporary digital tools, producing textile works that carry the physical warmth of handmade weaving while drawing on algorithmic pattern generation. Her practice asks what gets preserved — and what gets transformed — when traditional making meets machine logic.

Creative Boom

The tension here is interesting: tapestry was always a technology, a system for encoding complex imagery thread by thread. Holst isn’t abandoning the tradition — she’s extending it into a new material vocabulary. The craft question and the digital question turn out to be the same question.
Creative Practice The Single Most Important Thing You Can Do for Your Art: A Daily Practice

Establishing a consistent daily practice of just 20 minutes is highlighted as crucial for artists to build momentum and develop a strong artistic vision. The focus is on the act of making marks daily — not consuming art or planning — but actually touching the work.

Art2Life Industry Are Creatives Really Leaving the UK in Droves, and If So, Why?

A reported exodus of creative workers from the UK is prompting hard questions about cost of living, funding cuts, and the contraction of arts infrastructure. The piece maps where they’re heading and what it signals for the health of creative industries in Britain.

Creative Boom Essay The Biggest Threats to Creatives in 2026 and How to Protect Creative Focus

AI pressure, algorithm-driven homogenization, and burnout are identified as the three forces most actively eroding creative independence right now. The piece proposes concrete defenses: define your art’s meaning, protect creative time, build community, and use technology as a tool rather than a compass.

We Thought AI Feedback Was Making Our Designers Faster. It Was Making Them Shallower.

A design team’s honest post-mortem: AI-assisted critique loops sped up iteration but gradually eroded the designers’ capacity for independent judgment. The work got done faster; the designers got worse. A sharp, uncomfortable finding about what skill development actually requires.

UX Collective

The speed trap is real. When AI gives you feedback in seconds, you stop sitting with the discomfort that produces real growth. This piece names something most teams won’t admit yet — and the implications for how we train junior designers are significant.
Branding Beinbe: Inner Beauty Branding by Xolve

Studio Xolve’s identity work for Beinbe takes inner beauty as both subject and design system — soft forms, considered typography, and a restrained palette that communicates without overselling. Clean brand thinking for a category that usually shouts.

Abduzeedo Design Tactile Design: Texture and Touch in Modern Branding

A roundup of branding work leaning into tactile qualities — paper grain, embossing, fabric simulation, material texture — as a counterweight to the flat digital default. In a screen-first world, physical material references carry increasing visual weight.

Abduzeedo Trends UI Design Trends 2026: Patterns Shaping Modern Websites

Modular information architecture, bento grids, and motion as meaningful feedback are identified as the dominant UI patterns of 2026. Dark mode is no longer a toggle — it’s becoming the foundational design choice from which light variants are derived.
