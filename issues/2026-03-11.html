Paint & Canvas — Lead
Ludovic Nkoth on Why He Paints the ‘Emotional Texture of Everyday Scenarios’

Painter Ludovic Nkoth discusses his figurative work, which balances visual simplicity with conceptual weight. Drawing on Black and diasporic experience, his canvases probe the psychological edge between individual feeling and universal recognition — every face carrying a story that refuses easy reading.

Artnet News

What Nkoth is doing — making paintings that look approachable until they don’t — is a difficult trick. The emotional complexity is in the surface, not hidden beneath it. This is the kind of figurative work that actually earns its place in the conversation right now.
Exhibition The Whitney Biennial Is Here

The 2026 Whitney Biennial opens, bringing together artists whose work grapples with the current political and cultural moment in America. The survey arrives as one of the most anticipated in recent years, with a curatorial premise centered on resistance and reinvention.

Hyperallergic Animation / Sculpture An Animated Look at Noguchi’s Experimental Playgrounds That Were Never Built

A new animation resurrects Isamu Noguchi’s visionary playground designs from the mid-20th century, all of which were rejected or never realized. The work reveals how far ahead Noguchi was thinking — sculptural environments designed for children that felt more like art installations than anything built at the time.

Colossal Criticism Please, No More Disaffected White Girls

A sharp critical essay takes aim at the recurring figure of alienated young white womanhood that has saturated a particular vein of contemporary painting and photography. The piece asks what it means that this archetype keeps getting celebrated while adjacent subjects go underfunded.

A Living Spiral of Becoming: An Interview with Lily Kwong

Landscape artist Lily Kwong talks about how motherhood cracked open her creative practice — making her more fearless, more experimental, and more willing to sit with not-knowing. She describes her approach to art as a living system, something that grows and decays rather than resolves.

Autre

The idea that deep life change doesn’t interrupt a creative practice but fundamentally reorganizes it is something artists rarely talk about with this kind of honesty. Kwong does. Worth reading if you’ve ever felt your work shift underneath you and not known why.
Interview Glory Edim on Cultivating Talent in Yourself and Others

Glory Edim, founder of Well-Read Black Girl, walks through how she built a creative livelihood from community rather than solitude. She pushes back hard on the myth that true artistry requires isolation, arguing instead for collaborative creation as both ethic and practice.

The Creative Independent Essay Reflections from an Artist: How to Develop Your Own Personal Style

An MFA student shares what she’s learned about style development through continuous experimentation and the willingness to fail without shame. The piece is an honest look at the process from inside the work — not from the other side of it.

Tech × Art
Tech × Art — Lead
Sarah Rosalena’s “Threading the Infinite: Omnidirectional Terrain”

Artist Sarah Rosalena discusses her monumental textile work at LACMA, a piece that blends traditional weaving with digital media concepts to explore the interconnectedness of land, cosmos, and materials. The work pushes hard at the boundary between technology and craft — asking what it means to encode a landscape into thread.

LACMA Unframed

Weaving has always been a computational act — pattern as algorithm, warp and weft as binary logic. What Rosalena does is make that kinship explicit while still producing something physically overwhelming. The scale and the intimacy coexist. Genuinely interesting work.
Research Stanford Scholars Train Generative AI to Be Better Creative Collaborators

Stanford researchers are building open-source tools to improve human-AI collaboration in visual content creation by developing shared “conceptual grounding” between artist and model. The aim is giving artists more precise, honest control over generative systems rather than the prompt-guessing game most tools require.

Stanford HAI Digital Art Ronen Tanchum’s Human Atmospheres

Artist Ronen Tanchum creates interactive digital works and simulated weather environments that translate human emotional states into atmospheric phenomena. His practice treats technology’s fragility not as a bug but as the subject — the synthetic standing in for the natural, both equally temporary.

Interface & UX — Lead
What Happened to the Car Designed for Women, by Women?

A look back at Volvo’s YCC concept — a fully realized car designed entirely by a female team in 2004, which introduced features now standard across the industry, then quietly disappeared. The story is really about who gets to define what a product is for, and what happens when that power briefly shifts.

UX Collective

The YCC story is a design history lesson that cuts. A project that got everything right — process, outcome, reception — and still got filed away. If you care about participatory design and who gets left out of it, this is worth your time.
Tools Figma MCP Tutorial: How AI Tools Automate Your Design Workflow

A walkthrough of Figma’s Model Context Protocol integration, showing where AI automation saves real time in design workflows and where it still falls short. More honest about the gaps than most tool tutorials.

Abduzeedo Branding The Alpha Men Brand Identity by Clever Lemons

Studio Clever Lemons delivers a brand identity for Alpha Men that sidesteps the usual masculine clichés in favor of something quieter and more considered. The typography does most of the heavy lifting and earns it.
