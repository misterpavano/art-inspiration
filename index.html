Nostalgia and Decay Meet Theatricality in Andrew Moore’s Dramatic Photos

Andrew Moore has spent decades photographing the interiors of abandoned theaters and grand public spaces across America and Cuba, finding in decay a kind of concentrated beauty that the buildings never quite had when they were functional. The images are operatic and controlled at once — carefully composed to let the collapse speak without editorializing over it.

Colossal

Moore is working in a tradition of ruin photography that could easily tip into melancholy tourism, but the scale and formality of his approach keeps it on the right side of the line. These pictures are about architecture and time, not sentiment — a distinction worth thinking about for anyone interested in the relationship between subject and frame.
Drawing & Illustration Pejac Transforms Basic Graph Paper into Detailed, Trompe-L’œil Tableaux

Spanish street artist Pejac uses the grid of graph paper not as constraint but as visual scaffolding, drawing hyper-detailed graphite scenes that play with the paper’s geometry to create depth and optical illusion. The work is a precision exercise in using what’s already there rather than overriding it.

Colossal Sculpture & Animation Folklore and Nature Converge in Cat Johnston’s Expressive, Eccentric Puppets

Cat Johnston’s handmade puppets and paper sculptures pull from folklore traditions and natural history simultaneously, landing somewhere between taxidermy cabinet and fairy tale. The craft is immaculate and the sensibility genuinely strange — these objects feel like they arrived from somewhere specific that doesn’t exist on any map.

Colossal Museums & Institutions What Do We Really Think of the New New Museum?

Hyperallergic gathers critical perspectives on the recently expanded New Museum, examining whether the new building delivers on the institution’s founding commitment to emerging and underrepresented artists or has traded that identity for scale. The responses are varied enough to be useful and pointed enough to actually say something.

Obituaries & Legacy Remembering Axel Burrough, Kazumasa Nagai, and Éliane Radigue

Three practitioners who defined their fields: graphic designer Kazumasa Nagai, whose visual language shaped Japanese modernism; composer Éliane Radigue, a pioneer of drone and electronic music; and painter Axel Burrough. Hyperallergic’s remembrance treats their work with the same care they brought to making it.

A curated guide to Asia Art Week in New York, covering gallery shows, auction previews, and institutional programming across the city. Hyperallergic Personal & Place Flying Back With the Birds to My Hometown of Tehran

An artist’s reflection on return — to Tehran, to memory, to the specific visual and spatial logic of a city that shaped their sensibility. The piece is essayistic and precise, using birds as a structural device for thinking about migration, belonging, and how place persists in the body long after you’ve left it.

More Fire. More Fox. Meet Kit: Firefox’s Most Significant Brand Evolution in Years

Mozilla has introduced Kit, a new brand identity system for Firefox that leans into the original fox-and-flame mythology with more aggression and visual confidence than the browser has shown in over a decade. The redesign is a deliberate statement — Firefox knows it’s the underdog, and has decided to make that position into an identity rather than apologize for it.

Creative Boom

Brand evolutions this confident are rare in mature consumer software. The decision to double down on the creature rather than abstract it into a generic tech-brand mark is a creative call that could easily have gone the other way — and Creative Boom’s coverage is worth reading for the behind-the-scenes reasoning on why it didn’t.
Typography & Type Design 8 Eye-Catching Typefaces That Will Elevate Your Editorial Designs

Creative Boom’s type roundup surfaces eight typefaces built for the specific demands of editorial contexts — long-form reading, headline hierarchy, caption work — with enough character to carry a publication’s voice without overwhelming it. A practical shortlist, not a greatest-hits clip show.

Creative Boom Branding & Craft How Thirst Gave East London Whisky Its Edge

Design studio Thirst built the brand identity for East London Liquor Company’s whisky line, creating a visual system that communicates provenance and attitude without leaning on the heritage clichés that dominate the category. The case study is detailed on process — how the brief evolved, what got rejected, what landed.

Creative Boom Brand Strategy From Symbols to Systems: Designing Brands That Hold Under Pressure

A strategic argument for why brand design should be approached as systems architecture rather than symbol creation — the logo is the least important part of a brand that actually functions across every context it gets deployed into. The piece is direct about where most brand projects fail and why the failure is usually a scoping problem, not a craft one.

HEX’s Geometric Block System Gives Grafana a Visual Language for Its Products

Design studio HEX developed a modular visual identity system for Grafana built around geometric blocks — a formal language that scales from icon to billboard while keeping product family coherence intact. The system is designed to be extensible as Grafana’s product line grows, which puts the design work in a different register than a simple rebrand.

Abduzeedo

What makes this worth studying is the constraint logic: geometric modularity in brand systems is easy to execute poorly and hard to execute well. HEX’s approach is worth unpacking for anyone building visual systems that need to stay coherent across multiple products with different audiences and use cases.
Type Design Arha Typeface: Geometric Sans with Constructivist Roots

Arha is a geometric sans-serif typeface drawing explicitly from early 20th century Constructivist design principles — precise angles, rational proportions, forms that feel like they were derived from a system rather than drawn by hand. The result is a display face with genuine historical intelligence behind it, not just retro aesthetics.

Abduzeedo Branding & Identity Energy Branding for Nertik by Mubien Brands

Mubien Brands’ identity work for Nertik navigates the energy sector’s visual clichés — lightning bolts, gradient greens, aggressive diagonals — and arrives somewhere more considered. The system is restrained without being cold, which is genuinely hard to pull off in a category that rewards noise.
