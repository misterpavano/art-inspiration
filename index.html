'Lettres Décoratives' Is a Celebration of Fin de Siècle Sign Painters' Vibrant Letterforms

A newly published book resurrects the ornate, hand-lettered sign-painting tradition of late 19th-century France, showcasing chromolithographed alphabets dripping with Art Nouveau flourishes. For working painters, it's a reminder that the line between craft and fine art has always been thinner than galleries pretend.

Colossal

Sign painting was once the dominant public art form. These letterforms didn't hang in museums — they competed for your attention on the street, in color, at scale. The book makes the case that the best of them still hold up.
Sculpture Grant Garmezy Molds a Full-Size Dakotaraptor from Molten Glass

Glassblower Grant Garmezy spent months constructing a life-size Dakotaraptor entirely from blown and cast glass, each feather and claw shaped by hand at 2000°F. The result is both paleontologically detailed and luminous in a way fossils never are.

Why the Creative Industry Feels Broken Right Now (and Why It Isn't)

Creative Boom examines the collective exhaustion hitting agencies, freelancers, and studios — the sense that budgets are tighter, AI is eating entry-level work, and clients are harder to please than ever. The counterargument: every generation thought the industry was dying, and the people who kept making work were the ones who rebuilt it.

Creative Boom
