Featured — Exhibition
Whitney Biennial, Can You Hear Us?

Hyperallergic takes a critical look at the latest Whitney Biennial, questioning whether the institution is truly listening to the artists and communities it claims to represent. The piece examines the gap between curatorial ambition and the lived realities of the artists on display.

Hyperallergic

The essay positions the Biennial as a litmus test for how American art institutions handle political urgency, asking whether the show amplifies voices or merely curates the appearance of listening.
Drawing David Morrison’s Alluring Drawings Spring from the Blank Page

David Morrison creates intricate botanical drawings that emerge with startling precision from unmarked white paper, treating negative space as an active compositional element. Each drawing builds slowly from the center outward, rewarding patience with detail that makes you forget the page was ever blank.

Colossal Art World Marica Vilcek, Graceful Champion of Immigrant Artists, Dies at 89

Marica Vilcek spent decades supporting immigrant artists in the United States through the Vilcek Foundation, quietly shaping the landscape of contemporary American art from behind the scenes. Her legacy is measured not in the art she made but in the careers she helped build.

Hyperallergic Sculpture Painterly Figures Entwine in Soojin Choi’s Ceramic Sculptures

Soojin Choi sculpts intertwined figures in clay that capture the tension between intimacy and entanglement, with surfaces painted in loose, expressive brushwork. The sculptures feel like frozen moments from paintings that decided to step off the canvas and occupy real space.

Colossal
Photography How Photography Helped Build the Atomic Bomb

Hyperallergic examines the little-discussed role photography played in the Manhattan Project, from documentation to propaganda to the visual record of destruction. The piece raises uncomfortable questions about the camera as a tool of complicity, capturing scientific achievement and human catastrophe in the same frame.

Hyperallergic Criticism How to Survive AI

Hyperallergic explores how artists are navigating the disruption of generative AI, arguing that survival means neither blind adoption nor reflexive rejection but a more nuanced reckoning with what creative labor actually is. The piece profiles artists on both sides of the divide.

Hyperallergic Photography Frank Relle’s Photos Revel in Louisiana’s Otherworldly Swampland

Frank Relle photographs Louisiana bayous at night, using artificial light to transform familiar swampland into scenes that look like they belong on another planet. The long exposures reveal textures and colors in the water and canopy that daylight would flatten into green-brown monotony.

Colossal Illustration Ambiguity Reigns in Olaf Hajek’s Mysterious Illustrations

Olaf Hajek builds surreal narrative worlds where human figures coexist with botanical forms and mythological fragments, all rendered in rich, saturated color. The illustrations resist easy interpretation, inviting viewers to construct their own stories from the visual clues scattered across each composition.

Featured — Design Theory
Disruption has a shape. Design history shows us what it is.

UX Collective explores how design disruption follows recognizable visual patterns throughout history, from the Bauhaus to the smartphone era. The essay argues that truly disruptive design doesn’t just change aesthetics but reshapes the physical and conceptual space around it.

UX Collective

By mapping recurring formal signatures across decades of design upheaval, the piece gives designers a framework for recognizing disruption as it emerges rather than only in retrospect.
Design Thinking Taste is not a feature

This essay argues that taste in design has been mischaracterized as a deliverable or skill, when it actually functions as a form of accumulated judgment that resists systematization. It pushes back against the idea that good design can be reduced to frameworks and checklists.

UX Collective Design Tools Google Stitch, design maturity guide, livable products

UX Collective rounds up three significant developments: Google’s new Stitch prototyping tool, an updated framework for measuring design maturity, and the concept of livable products. The common thread is a shift toward tools that treat design as an ongoing relationship with users.

UX Collective Branding Harvard COOP Gets a New Brand Identity

Abduzeedo showcases the new brand identity for Harvard COOP, the university’s historic campus store, balancing institutional weight with contemporary retail sensibility. The rebrand preserves legacy cues while modernizing the visual system for a new generation of students and visitors.

Abduzeedo
Industry The mirage of UX Design’s demise keeps coming back

UX Collective tackles the recurring narrative that UX design is dying, arguing that each wave of disruption triggers the same obituary and each time the field absorbs the change. The discipline keeps evolving rather than disappearing, coming out differently shaped but very much alive.

UX Collective 3D & AI Meshy-6 AI Image to 3D Now Live on MakerWorld

Abduzeedo covers the launch of Meshy-6, an AI tool that converts 2D images into printable 3D models, now integrated directly into MakerWorld. The tool lowers the barrier between digital design and physical fabrication to essentially a single click.

Abduzeedo Design & Fabrication Inorganic Growth: 3D Printed Street Furniture from Urban Rubble

Abduzeedo profiles a project that transforms construction waste into 3D-printed street furniture, turning demolition debris into functional public seating. The work sits at the intersection of circular economy thinking and computational design, proving that rubble can have a second life as civic infrastructure.

Abduzeedo Design Systems Design Systems in Practice with Figma: Part 1 — The Foundations

This deep dive covers the foundational phase of building robust design systems in Figma, focusing on strategic implementation of design tokens for colors and spacing. It emphasizes how these building blocks replace generic values with meaningful names, ensuring consistency across digital products.

How ‘Parasite’ Made One of the Greatest Montages Ever

No Film School breaks down the montage sequence in Bong Joon-ho’s Parasite, examining how editing rhythm, score, and spatial choreography combine to create one of modern cinema’s most effective sequences.

No Film School

The analysis reveals how the montage compresses class dynamics into pure visual storytelling, advancing plot and theme simultaneously without a wasted frame.
Screenwriting How Did They Write ‘The Wire’?

No Film School digs into the writing process behind The Wire, exploring how David Simon and his team built one of television’s most complex narrative architectures. Journalism-trained writers brought documentary rigor to fiction, treating Baltimore itself as a character with its own arc across five seasons.

No Film School Behind the Scenes Inside That Bloody ‘Something Very Bad Is Going to Happen’ Wedding Day Finale

IndieWire goes behind the scenes of the blood-soaked finale of Something Very Bad Is Going to Happen, unpacking how the creative team orchestrated the show’s most visceral sequence. The interview reveals the logistical and emotional challenges of staging horror within the confines of a wedding narrative.

IndieWire Midnight Movie Stressed Out at Work? Simmer Down with 1988’s ‘Death Spa’

IndieWire revisits the 1988 horror film Death Spa as a midnight movie worth rediscovering, finding unexpected charm in its combination of workplace anxiety and supernatural mayhem. The film’s premise of a killer health club has aged into something weirdly prescient about our obsession with wellness culture.
